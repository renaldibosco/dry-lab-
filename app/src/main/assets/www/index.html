Open a FASTA or GenBank file, or paste a sequence. Dry Lab finds the genes, shows the ATG start and the stop codon, and makes your PCR template and primers.

1. Your DNA sequence

Paste DNA, or open a FASTA (.fasta, .fa), GenBank (.gb) or text file. Numbers and spaces are removed for you. RNA (U) is changed to DNA (T).

G + C (3 bonds, holds tighter) A + T (2 bonds)

GC content matters for heat: G-C pairs have 3 hydrogen bonds, A-T pairs have 2. More GC means the DNA needs more heat to separate (a higher melting temperature).

GC along the sequence

Peaks above the red line (65%) are GC-rich spots. They are harder to copy by PCR and may need 98 °C, DMSO or betaine.

DNA map

Arrows are genes, pointing the way they are read. Enzymes that cut only once are named: they are the useful ones for opening a plasmid. Blue bars show more GC than average, orange bars less. Plasmids are circles: choose Circular for them.

2. Genes: start and stop codons

An open reading frame (ORF) starts at a start codon (ATG) and runs in steps of 3 bases until a stop codon (TAA, TAG or TGA). Genes can be on either strand, so both are searched.

Analyze a sequence first.

Arrows on top read left to right (+ strand). Arrows below read right to left (- strand).

3. PCR primers

Primers are short DNA pieces (18-25 bases) that mark where copying starts. Good primers have Tm 55-65 °C, GC 40-60%, one or two G/C at the 3' end, and do not stick to themselves.

Settings (primer rules and reaction salts)

Cloning add-on: restriction sites on the primer ends

Adds an enzyme site plus 4 extra bases (GCGC) to the 5' end, so the PCR product can be cut and pasted into a plasmid. The extra bases do not change the binding Tm shown.

4. PCR program: temperatures and times

Each cycle has three temperatures: very hot to separate the DNA strands (denaturation), cooler so primers can stick (annealing), then 72 °C where the polymerase copies (extension). Annealing temperature (Ta) comes from the primers' Tm.

Watch PCR copy your DNA

Every cycle doubles the DNA: 1 copy becomes 2, then 4, 8, 16... After 30 cycles one molecule becomes about a billion copies, enough to see as a band on a gel.

Master mix calculator

Make one mix for all tubes plus a little extra (pipetting loses some), then add template DNA to each tube. Always include a no-template control (water instead of DNA).

5. Virtual gel

Agarose gel electrophoresis separates DNA by size: small pieces run fast and travel far, big pieces stay near the wells. Compare your bands with the ladder to read their size.

Read my gel photo

Take a photo of your real gel. Tap the ladder lane, then tap each sample lane. Dry Lab finds the bands and measures their size from the ladder.

Restriction digest

Pick up to four enzymes. Dry Lab cuts your DNA on the computer and tells you how many pieces you will see on a gel, and what sticky ends they have.

Plasmid library

Real, checked plasmid sequences to practise cloning on. Use one as your sequence, cut it, or use it as the vector for an assembly. Add your own plasmid too.

Add my own plasmid

Assemble DNA pieces

Gibson joins pieces that share a matching end (an overlap). Dry Lab cuts your vector, adds the overlaps to the insert's primers, and checks that the pieces join into the right circle.

Golden Gate uses enzymes that cut outside their own site (BsaI, BsmBI, BbsI, SapI). Each part gets its own 4-base sticky end, so the parts can only join in one order. Dry Lab picks safe sticky ends and checks the result.

Codon optimizer

Different species prefer different codons for the same amino acid. This rewrites a gene with the codons your host likes best, without changing the protein, and removes enzyme sites you do not want.

Primer risk report

Checks whether a primer folds back on itself (hairpin) or sticks to itself or to its partner (dimer), and gives the energy of the worst case. The more negative, the stickier.

Exon mapper

Genes of plants, animals and fungi are cut into exons by introns. Give the genomic DNA and the spliced transcript (mRNA or CDS), and Dry Lab finds every exon, intron and splice signal, then designs qPCR primers that cross an exon junction so they do not copy genomic DNA.

Echo prompt

A small taste of Echo, the English-word language Renaldi is building. Type a line, for example: gc of ATGCGC, translate ATGGCCTAA, tm of GGATCCGATCGATCG, cut with EcoRI. Type help to see every word.
